Practical Arabic · Module 1

Greetings & Status

السلام عليكم

Assalamu Alaikum

Peace be upon you

Breakdown: Al-Salam (Peace) + 'Ala (Upon) + -kum (You)

كيف حالك؟

Kaifa haluk?

How are you?

Breakdown: Kaifa (How) + Hal (Condition) + -ka (Your)

أنا بخير، الحمد لله

Ana bikhair, Alhamdulillah

I am fine, praise be to Allah

Breakdown: Ana (I) + Bi-khair (In good) + Praise to Allah

Personal Identity

ما اسمك؟

Ma ismuk?

What is your name?

Ma (What) + Ism (Name) + -uk (Your)

اسمي ...

Ismi ...

My name is ...

Ism (Name) + -i (My)

Min aina anta?

Where are you from?

Min (From) + Aina (Where) + Anta (You)

I am from ...

Ana (I) + Min (From)

Practical Arabic · Module 3

Humanitarian Action

أعمل في المجال الإنساني

A'mal fil-majalil-insani

I work in the humanitarian field

A'mal (I work) + Fi (in) + Al-Majal (the field) + Al-Insani (humanitarian)

نحن نساعد المحتاجين

Nahnu nusa'idul muhtajin

We help people in need

Nahnu (We) + Nusa'id (help) + Al-Muhtajin (people in need)

يحتاج الناس إلى الطعام والماء

Yahtajunnas ilatta'am wal-maa

People need food and water

Yahtaju (Need) + Al-Nas (people) + Ila (to) + Al-Ta'am (food) + Wa (and) + Al-Ma' (water)

Arabic Foundations · Lesson 2

Joined Letters

These are joint letters. We still read every alphabet separately — never as one English-style word.

How to read

Look at the joined shape, then name each letter separately from right to left.

لا

Laam, Alif

Not “laa” as one word

بلب

Baa, Laam, Baa

Not “balb”

Read letter by letter

Tap a join to hear Alif, Baa, Laam… with a pause after each name.

Same letter, different drawing

Workbook handwriting and screen fonts look different on purpose, so you can still recognise Baa, Jeem, Kaaf, and the rest.

Practice the same way

Quizzes ask you to name the letters in order, not to pronounce a joined word.
